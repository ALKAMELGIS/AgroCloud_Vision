# Rebuild EOS's workspace from documented behaviour

AgroCloud Vision can rebuild a functional equivalent of the EOS Crop Monitoring workspace. The documented behaviour is enough to do it: a **field list with search, crop/group filters, six sort orders and NDVI-change risk tags**; a map with **coordinate search, distance/area measurement, a contrast toggle and a two-panel split view with synchronised cursor readout**; a **date line that hides images at or above 50% cloud by default**; six built-in indices (VMI, NDVI, NDRE, MSAVI, ReCI, NDMI); a field-analytics page with a ha/% distribution table, multi-year index curves, BBCH stages and weather charts; a Leaderboard that ranks fields by NDVI change between the two latest images; Season Analytics and column-picker Custom Reports; and four VRA map types that produce **2–7 zones (default 3, minimum zone area 1,000 m² in the API)**, take a per-zone rate per ha/acre, compare against a flat rate in a savings calculator, and export SHP presets (John Deere, Amazone, Trimble, universal) or ISO-XML. Scouting, the Field Activity Log and NDVI-change email notifications are documented down to field labels, statuses and colour codes. The gaps sit in three places. First, EOS's **22 July 2026 navigation redesign** renamed "Global view" to "Fields" and "Field Leaderboard" to "Leaderboard" and reorganised the menu, but the user guide still describes the older layout, so rail placement, the AI assistant, a Notifications panel and "check fields with risks" chips have no public documentation. Second, every algorithm (risk thresholds, zone clustering, Map Builder normalisation, auto rate distribution, soil-sampling grid defaults) is undisclosed. Third, export file schemas (DBF column names, ISO-XML grid vs polygon) are not published. The report below separates what EOS documents from what AgroCloud must choose, and gives a rebuild default for every undocumented parameter. All sources are public EOS pages and the iOS App Store listing; no logged-in session was used.

## The 2026 navigation is documented in outline but not on screen

EOS's guide lists the product modules as Work With Crop Map, Seasonality, Add field, Monitoring, Weather, Scouting, Overview (Season Analytics, Field Leaderboard, Custom Report), VRA maps, Field Activity Log, Data Manager, Field Manager (crop rotation, sowing, crop allocation, field groups), Team Management, Settings and API access ([EOS user guide index](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/)). The **22.07.2026 "Simpler Navigation" release** reshaped that menu. "Global view" became **"Fields"**, and Field Analytics now opens by selecting a field under Fields, with an **"All Fields" dropdown** for jumping between field pages. "Field Leaderboard" became **"Leaderboard"**, "Disease & Pest" stays inside Field Analytics, Marketplace and Data Manager left the side menu (Data Manager is still reachable when creating a VRA map), and Field Groups moved under Account → Team Management ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). For AgroCloud the practical structure is therefore a persistent navigation with **Fields** (map plus list), a per-field **Field Analytics** page, **Leaderboard**, **Overview/Reports**, **Weather**, **VRA maps** (Sowing, Vegetation, P&K, Map builder, Soil sampling), **Scouting**, **Field Activity Log** and account/team settings. Whether EOS puts this on a left menu or a right rail in 2026 could not be checked without logging in, and the guide's screenshots predate the redesign. That placement is AgroCloud's own design choice.

The field list is documented precisely. A **search box** finds fields (and scouting tasks) by name. **Filters** use crop-name and group-name checkboxes committed with an **APPLY** button, and the same filters apply on the Scouting tab. A sort control on the right of the list offers six orders: **Newest, Oldest, name A–Z, name Z–A, area Low to High, area High to Low**. Each **field card** shows the field name, area in ha, group (only if one is assigned), current crop and location (district and country), plus a **"Find field" button** that zooms the map to the field in any layer ([EOS: tools for fields and tasks](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/tools-to-work-with-fields-and-tasks/)).

Risk flagging came with the 2023 "Field Inspector" release. Each list item carries tags at its bottom edge. The risk tags are **"High NDVI change"** and **"Low NDVI change"**, and the status tags are **"No data"** (no recent satellite data), **"No risks"** (NDVI within normal limits) and **"Limited Access"**. A top-of-list alert about "fields that require attention" filters to low or high risks when clicked, and a **"Show only fields with risks"** option narrows the list. On the field page, a **"Field state changes"** block shows the number of risks, the NDVI change between the last two images, both image dates and their cloud cover ([EOS blog: risk tags](https://eos.com/blog/risk-tags-context-help-new-in-eosda-crop-monitoring)). A wider risk vocabulary appears as Custom Report columns: **Index, Disease, Cold stress, Hot stress, Rainfall and Wind risk** ([EOS Overview guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/overview/)). A 2024 **Risk Map** on the global view colours each field **No Risk, Low, Medium or High** for "Index Risk" and "Disease Risk" ([EOS blog, Jul 2024](https://eos.com/blog/updated-vra-maps-more-in-eosda-crop-monitoring/)). Field-level risks under Crop info need a paid plan, show a probability level and open details on click, and disease risk is a separate add-on ([EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/)).

The "Check fields with risks" chips with per-type icons named in AgroCloud's brief are most likely the 2026 form of that "fields that require attention" alert, with one chip per risk type matching the six report columns. That is an inference: **no EOS page describes chips, icons, colours or the NDVI-change threshold**. The rebuild rule should compare the field-mean NDVI of the last two valid images, tag "Low NDVI change" or "High NDVI change" when the absolute delta exceeds a configurable threshold, and tag "No data" when the newest valid image is older than a freshness window. Bulk actions in the list are also undocumented.

## The map, date line and field page share one index state

**Map controls.** The search box accepts a place name or coordinates, **longitude first, then latitude**. Zoom uses +/− buttons and the mouse wheel. A **"Measure distance" tool** draws a line for distance or outlines an area, and the result appears at the bottom of the screen ([EOS: work with crop map](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/work-with-crop-map)). The Fields map has a layers dropdown with five documented layers:

- **My Crops**: fields coloured by their most recently planted crop, with the legend at the lower right.
- **Vegetation**: average NDVI per field binned into **10 value ranges**, with a "Select group" filter and a bottom timeline. Clicking a field opens monitoring.
- **Water Stress**: average NDMI per field in 10 ranges.
- **Vegetation Rating**: fields ranked by average NDVI.
- **Crop Classification**: a national crop map, Ukraine only.

Global layers use only images under 90% cloud, mask cloudy areas, and switch from pins to field outlines as the user zooms in ([EOS: work with crop map](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/work-with-crop-map)). A **contrast icon at the lower right** toggles between "Standard" (a smooth gradient, e.g. NDVI −1..1 red–yellow–green, NDMI in blues) and "Contrast" (discrete colours that amplify small differences), and turns blue when active. Basemap options and a swipe slider are not documented, so a satellite/streets toggle is a rebuild choice.

**Split view** is EOS's compare tool, and it is a two-panel design, not a swipe. Opened from a side-menu icon for the selected field, it splits the screen into **two equal panels**. Each panel has its own timeline, index switcher, expandable legend and download button, and each defaults to NDVI on the most recent image. The calendar highlights available dates, history goes back five years, and **hovering a point shows its index value in both panels at once** ([EOS: work with crop map](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/work-with-crop-map)). Since 27.02.2025 split view also works on the global Fields view, comparing indices across periods for all fields at once ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). EOS recommends pairing an index with **Natural color** to check the cloud mask ([EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/)). The rebuild is two map instances with synchronised centre and zoom and a shared cursor readout. A swipe mode can be added, but EOS does not document one.

**The date line** at the bottom of the map lists every available image. Clicking a date renders it in the current index. Hovering shows a preview, the **cloud-plus-shadow percentage** and the acquisition time in **GMT**. By default, images with **50% or more cloud are hidden**, and the threshold is an account setting. A calendar icon picks the year. **The last three months are free**, and imagery back to **2016** needs a paid plan. Sentinel-2 at 10 m is the default source, and daily PlanetScope at 3 m can be connected. A **mask filter** has three checkboxes, clouds, cirrus and cloud shadows, all on by default, and masked pixels are excluded from index calculations ([EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/)). Chip layout, arrows and scrolling are undocumented. The natural rebuild is a chip of {date, source badge, cloud %} with a lock icon when the date falls outside the plan's history window.

**Indices.** The index dropdown offers **VMI, NDVI, NDRE, MSAVI, ReCI and NDMI**, and NDVI is the default. VMI is an RGB composite (R = MSAVI, G = NDRE, B = NDVI) built on absolute values so its colours stay comparable across crops, regions and dates. The guide assigns each index a use: NDVI for general vigour, MSAVI for early emergence over bare soil, NDRE for mid-to-late nitrogen, ReCI for chlorophyll and yellowing, and NDMI for water stress ([EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/)). EOS's formula page gives NDVI = (NIR−RED)/(NIR+RED), NDRE = (NIR−RedEdge)/(NIR+RedEdge) and the standard MSAVI square-root form ([EOS blog: vegetation indices](https://eos.com/blog/vegetation-indices/)). It writes ReCI as (NIR/RED)−1. The standard definition uses red-edge, so implement **(NIR/RedEdge)−1** and treat EOS's version as a likely typo. NDMI on Sentinel-2 is (B8−B11)/(B8+B11) ([EOS NDMI](https://eos.com/make-an-analysis/ndmi/)). EOS does not say which red-edge band it uses. B5 (the 20 m band nearest red) is a defensible default. Elevation (dark green low to dark red high, metres on hover, .tiff download) and slope (degrees, red steep to green gentle) sit in the source dropdown rather than the index list. Index maps download as **.tiff (rendered) or .shp (per-pixel values)**, along with field contours ([EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/)). EOS also documents add-on indices: EVI, SAVI, ARVI, GCI, SIPI, NDWI, GNDVI, OSAVI and VARI ([EOS blog: vegetation indices](https://eos.com/blog/vegetation-indices/)).

**Field Analytics** combines these panels around one field:

- **Crop info**: crop rotation (empty until a crop and sowing date are entered; several crops per season, switchable), the current **BBCH growth stage** with start dates (some crops need a manual start via "edit growth stage"), risks, and the yield add-on.
- **Details**: index value classes in **hectares or percent**, with XLS download.
- **Vegetation index chart**: one curve per year, each hidden or shown via its coloured legend button.
- **Weather chart**: a dropdown for Temperature (daily min/max, **cold threat −6 °C**, **heat stress +30 °C**) or Moisture (precipitation in mm, root-zone and surface soil moisture %), on a one-year default period that "Update" resets.

All of the above come from the [EOS Monitoring guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/fields/). Growth stages divide indices into "recommended" and "additional" per crop. A 2025 **"Detection of sown area"** metric reports the uniformity and emergence of annual crops and feeds Custom Reports ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). The Details class boundaries and the summary statistics shown (mean, min, max, median) are not documented. A rebuild should compute a histogram over the polygon, report area as pixel count × pixel area (100 m² at 10 m), and show mean and percentiles.

## Leaderboard, reports and weather all aggregate per-field statistics

**Leaderboard.** It ranks fields by the **NDVI change between the two latest images** before a chosen **"Report date"**. Those images are typically 3–5 days apart. The default ordering takes the latest image and puts the most negative change first, and an **"NDVI Drop"** view ranks by change alone. Columns, each sortable with one active sort, are **Name, Location, Area, Group, Crop, Index value, Value change and Image date**. Change is shown in **red with a minus for drops, green with a plus for rises, and white for no change**. Filters cover all groups, fields without a group, a specific group, and crop checkboxes (these need crop-rotation data). Output is **PDF and XLS export**, plus an email after each new image listing up to three top fields with index, change, name, location and both image dates ([EOS Overview guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/overview/); [EOS Notification manager](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/notification-manager)). The guide variously mentions "7 leaderboards" and "9 lists" without listing them. The safe reading is one ranking per index and view.

**Reports** come in two documented forms. **Season Analytics** is a fixed dashboard:

- A header with the season name, its dates, and the count and area of full vs limited-access fields.
- A Crops widget with a per-crop Risks indicator that lists affected fields.
- Sown area by crop.
- Activity-log counts by status (Planned, In progress, Overdue, Completed).
- Planned vs actual activity costs.
- **Weekly Crop Performance**: average NDVI per week since the earliest sowing, with average, max and min on hover.
- **Top-10 best and worst fields** against their crop's average NDVI.

**Custom Report** is a template builder ("Create template", "Edit", "Update data", "Delete") with crop and group filters and reorderable, hideable columns. "Field" and "Crop" are fixed. The column groups are:

- **Crop rotation**: crop, maturity, variety, sowing and harvest dates, target and actual yield in t/ha.
- **Field**: name, group, area, tillage type, irrigation type.
- **Per index** (NDVI, NDRE, MSAVI, ReCI, NDMI): last image date, last value, change vs previous.
- **Yield-estimation add-on**: dry biomass, dry/wet yield, recommended harvest date.
- **Risks**: the six types listed earlier.

A report takes from seconds to an hour to generate ([EOS Overview guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/overview/)). For AgroCloud, the custom report is a column-picker over a per-field statistics table, and it should be generated asynchronously.

**Weather analytics** keeps history **back to 2008**, offers **"Compare with 5 years average"**, and charts accumulated and daily precipitation, daily min/max temperature, and a **sum of active temperatures with a selectable base of 0, 5 or 10 °C**. The forecast runs **14 days** with wind, humidity, cloud cover and precipitation. **Hourly spraying and tillage suitability markers** are coloured green, orange or red from air temperature, humidity, wind, forecast rain, rain over the last 24/48/72 h, soil moisture and soil temperature ([EOS Weather guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/weather/)). EOS publishes neither the active-temperature formula nor the suitability thresholds. The standard GDD form, Σ max(0, Tmean − base) from the sowing date, is the rebuild default, and the spray-window thresholds must be set by AgroCloud's agronomists.

## VRA maps share one zoning engine with four input recipes

EOS renamed its zoning module in Q2 2024: "Zoning" became **VRA maps**, "Vegetation maps" became "Nitrogen fertilization maps", "Multilayer maps" became **Map builder**, and "Productivity maps" split into **Sowing** and **P&K** maps. Choosing a type opens a field list showing how many maps each field already has. The editor has **"Parameters" and "Zones" tabs**, and a soil-erosion layer now enters the calculation ([EOS blog, Jul 2024](https://eos.com/blog/updated-vra-maps-more-in-eosda-crop-monitoring/)). The marketing names in 2026 are Sowing, Vegetation, P&K fertilization and Map builder, and every flow ends in **"Calculate"** ([EOS VRA page](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/key-functions/variable-rate-technology/)).

| Map type | Input imagery | Key parameters | Documented defaults and behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sowing** | Multi-year NDVI over a user-chosen period (from 2016); all cloud-free images, anomalies excluded; PlanetScope since 11.04.2025 | Period, zone count | 2–7 zones, default 3; "no more than a minute"; leave out an unharvested current year |
| **P&K** | Same productivity recipe as Sowing | Period, zone count | Same as Sowing; guide text duplicates the sowing page |
| **Vegetation (Nitrogen)** | One recent image; NDVI, NDRE, ReCI or MSAVI (API also lists NDMI) | Index, "Date" (latest recommended), detail level | Seconds to calculate; opacity slider default **80%**; green = higher, red = lower; detail max/medium/min for small/average/large fields |
| **Map builder** | Up to **5 layers**: NDVI, NDRE, MSAVI, ReCI, NDMI, elevation, custom indices, uploaded yield/seeding files; S2 or PlanetScope (21.02.2025) | Zones 2–7 (3–5 suggested), detail, per-layer date and weight | Weights are ratios (NDVI 100% + elevation 50% = 2:1); opacity is display only; **Zone 1 = highest** |

Sources for the table: [EOS user guide: Zoning](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/zoning), [EOS VRA page](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/key-functions/variable-rate-technology/), [EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/), and [EOS blog: Map builder](https://eos.com/blog/eosda-crop-monitoring-map-builder-feature-rolled-out/). In Map builder, machinery data enters through "Add layer" → "Uploaded files". The latest Data Manager file for the field is added automatically, "Activity and date" picks another file, and "Parameters" picks the attribute ([EOS user guide: Zoning](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/zoning)). Data Manager accepts a **ZIP of SHP/SHX/DBF (with optional PRJ)**, and parameter names come from the file and cannot be edited ([EOS Data Manager](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/data-manager/)).

**The API reveals the hidden defaults.** `POST /zoning/productivity-map` takes `vegetation_index` (default NDVI), **`zone_quantity` 2–7 (default 3)**, **`min_zone_area` in m² (default 1000)**, `date_from`/`date_to` (default the last 365 days) and `dataset_id` (default `sentinel2`), and runs asynchronously in about 30–180 s ([EOS API: productivity map](https://doc.eos.com/docs/zoning/productivity-map/)). The vegetation-map endpoint swaps the period for `image_date` and returns a **`kmean`** attribute on each zone ([EOS API: vegetation map](https://doc.eos.com/docs/zoning/vegetation-map/)). Output carries `type_zmap`, `vegetation_index`, `date` and `total_fertilizer_consumption`, and each zone has `zone_area`, `zone_p`, `fertilizer` (the rate) and one GeoJSON **MultiPolygon**. A separate endpoint returns a zipped SHP ([EOS Zoning API](https://doc.eos.com/docs/zoning/)). The API docs are internally inconsistent (zones numbered from 0 in one example and from 1 in another, `zone_p` described as a percentage but holding values near 12,302) and still call ISO-XML and savings "soon", so treat them as a guide to defaults, not a schema to copy.

**Rates and savings.** The user enters an amount **per 1 ha or acre for each zone**, and the system shows each zone's total in the chosen unit. The **"Total savings calculator"** takes a "Price per one unit" (currency follows the account location) and a flat rate, and outputs the flat-rate total, the flat-rate budget, **"Fertilizer saved"** and **"Total savings"** ([EOS user guide: Zoning](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/zoning)). In mid-2025 EOS added **automatic rate distribution from a specified average**, first for vegetation maps (24.06.2025) and then for sowing and macro-fertiliser maps (01.07.2025) ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). The mapping from index to rate is not published. A rebuild should scale each zone's rate by its relative mean index so the area-weighted mean equals the target. It should also offer **direct** (more input on strong zones) and **inverse** (more N on weak zones) modes, because the agronomic direction depends on the input.

**Export.** "EXPORT" opens a format dropdown. Hovering a format offers "Download this map" or **"Get link to this map" (valid 10 days)**. The SHP presets are **John Deere, Amazone, Trimble** (CFX‑750/FM‑750, FmX/FM‑1000, CFX‑350, GFX‑750, TMX‑2050) and **universal SHP**, plus **ISO-XML** for ISOBUS. Map builder exports are labelled "Multilayer" ([EOS user guide: Zoning](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/zoning)). EOS publishes no DBF column names, unit handling, or ISO-XML zone structure. AgroCloud should therefore follow general format rules. DBF field names are limited to **10 characters**, and a ZIP holds .shp/.dbf/.shx/.prj under one base name ([Leaf file preparation](https://docs.withleaf.io/guides/planning/file-preparation)). ISO-XML setpoint mass is **DDI 6 in mg/m²**, so 150 kg/ha is written as 15,000 ([isobus.net DDI list](https://www.isobus.net/isobus/dDEntity)).

**Clustering is inferred, not documented.** The `kmean` attribute and the `min_zone_area` default point to k-means classes on the index raster, vectorised, with polygons under 0.1 ha merged away. "Detail level" most plausibly scales the smoothing kernel and minimum area together. For Map builder, a defensible implementation resamples layers to a common grid, normalises each one, applies weight/Σweights, and clusters the stack. EOS does not say whether it normalises layers or inverts any of them (for example, treating high elevation as worse).

## Field operations tools are documented at form level, not data level

**Soil sampling** arrived on **27.03.2025** as a tool under VRA maps. It generates sampling maps "based on zone size, with sampling points spread evenly across one field or a whole farm" ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). The flow is: open the Soil sampling tool, choose a field division method (**by area or by number of zones**), pick **"Grid sampling"** from a "Points distribution methods" dropdown, and generate points with GPS coordinates. Maps export as **SHP or KML**, and lab results return via Data Manager to drive a new VRA map ([EOS blog: grid soil sampling](https://eos.com/blog/grid-soil-sampling/)). The same post cites grid cells of about **1 ha or 0.6 ha with 10–15 cores per cell** as general agronomy, not as platform defaults. Extension guidance sets one sample per 1 acre for best accuracy and never coarser than one per 2.5 acres ([UNL EC154](https://extensionpubs.unl.edu/publication/ec154/2009/pdf/view/ec154-2009.pdf)). EOS publishes no default cell size, point count, label scheme, PDF output or lab template. A rebuild should offer cell sizes of 0.5/1/2.5/5 ha or a zone-based mode reusing a Sowing map, place points at cell centroids inside an inward buffer, label them per field (e.g. `F012-G07`), and join lab CSVs on the point ID.

**Scout tasks** are map pins assigned to team members. The task has a **"General" tab** (name, description, field photo, close) and a **"Report" tab** completed by the scout:

- Inspection date, client (field owner) and field number.
- Editable area, crop, hybrid and sowing date.
- Growth stages (root thickness, leaf count) and plant density.
- A final review of crop condition with an **expert comment**.

"Export" downloads the report as a spreadsheet, and closed tasks move to a **"Closed" tab** and show as closed on the map ([EOS Scouting guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/scouting/)). The mobile app supplies the field side, with **offline maps**, a **threat type (fungi, pests, diseases, weeds, other)** with photos, assignee and reassignment, **due dates**, **"Check-in"**, and notifications for assigned and overdue tasks ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1474361532)). The documented creation flow is to "put a pin" on a problem area seen in the imagery and assign a scout, who is notified at once ([EOS blog: scout crops](https://eos.com/blog/how-to/scout-crops/)). Roles govern access. **Admin** creates, edits and closes tasks and reports. **Scout** creates, assigns and completes. **Observer** creates tasks and completes only their own. Access can be limited to single fields, groups or all fields ([EOS Team management](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/user-guide/team-management)). Only open and closed statuses are documented, with "overdue" derived from the due date. Priority, multi-point tasks and comment threads are not documented. Storing the index and image date that the pin was placed on lets the scout see the same view, and EOS documents no automatic task creation from alerts.

**The Field Activity Log** is a calendar grid with three columns: Field (sortable A–Z/Z–A), Sowing date (sortable) and an activity calendar split by a **current-date column**, with completed work on the left and planned work on the right. Filters cover **year, field group, crop and activity type**. Activity types are **fertilizing, tillage, planting, spraying, harvesting and others**, each with a cost and an optional description. Planned activities take an **estimated cost** and completed ones an **actual cost**. The colour code ([EOS Field Activity Log guide](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/field-activity-log)) is:

- **Grey with stripes**: a planned single-day activity.
- **Red**: planned and past its start date without confirmation, or a day behind schedule.
- **Green**: completed.
- **Blue**: a multi-day activity in progress.
- **Yellow**: a past activity entered as already completed.

Since **26.02.2025**, dragging across the calendar adds one activity to several fields with a duration ([EOS What's new](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/whats-new/)). Machinery files imported through Data Manager become activities with equipment parameters such as yield or application rate ([EOS Data Manager](https://eos.com/user-guide/crop-monitoring/data-manager/)). EOS documents no product, rate or machinery fields and no log export. AgroCloud should add `inputs[]`, `machinery` and a `vra_map_id` link so a prescription can be traced to its application.

**Notifications** are documented as email-first. The **Notification manager** sends email when Sentinel-2 NDVI changes up or down beyond **"a five percentile margin"** by default, and also on each new image. It is on by default for every field, has a per-field **"Mute"**, an Index menu (NDVI only) and a "Value change" step. Its list shows footprint, name, region, coordinates, area, crop, current index, change and last image date ([EOS blog: field change monitoring](https://eos.com/blog/field-change-monitoring-with-regular-notifications-taking-care-of-users/)). That post is old, and its plan limits are stale. Other documented events are Leaderboard emails, a **"Weekly index change reports"** add-on (index drops or spikes over seven days plus precipitation and min/max temperature) ([EOS agriculture monitoring](https://eos.com/products/crop-monitoring/agriculture-monitoring/)), mobile notifications for assigned and overdue tasks and activities ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1474361532)), and a zoning-API email when a map finishes ([EOS API: productivity map](https://doc.eos.com/docs/zoning/productivity-map/)). **No public page describes an in-app Notifications panel for the 2026 web UI, weather-alert thresholds or per-event channel settings.** A rebuild catalogue of `new_image`, `index_change`, `risk_level_change`, `task_assigned`/`overdue`, `activity_overdue`, `report_submitted`, `export_ready` and `weather_risk` covers the documented events. Defaulting `weather_risk` to the chart markers (−6 °C, +30 °C) is AgroCloud's choice.

## What EOS documents and what AgroCloud must decide

| Area | Documented by EOS | Inferred or undocumented: AgroCloud decides |
|---|---|---|
| Navigation | Module list; 2026 renames (Fields, Leaderboard), All Fields dropdown | Rail position, icon set, AI assistant, Notifications panel |
| Field list | Search, crop/group filters + APPLY, 6 sorts, card fields, "Find field" | Bulk actions; chip design |
| Risks | High/Low NDVI change, No data, No risks, Limited Access; six risk types; No/Low/Medium/High risk map | ΔNDVI thresholds; risk-level cut-offs; chip icons |

## Conclusion

The workspace's architecture follows from these documents more than any single screen does. Nearly every EOS module (risk tags, Leaderboard, Vegetation Rating layer, Custom Report columns, notification emails, Season Analytics top-10) is a different view of one table: per-field, per-image index statistics plus the change between consecutive valid images. Zones are the second shared object, feeding Sowing, P&K, soil sampling and later rate maps. AgroCloud should therefore build two engines before any screen: a per-field statistics store keyed by (field, date, index, source), and a zoning service that returns MultiPolygon zones with area, mean and rate. With those two in place, most of the workspace is presentation, and EOS's labels, colour codes and defaults can be copied directly from the table above.

The undocumented parts all concern judgement: how large an NDVI change counts as a risk, how zones are clustered and smoothed, how an average rate is spread, and how a terminal expects its columns. These are also the settings most likely to differ between regions, so they should stay editable and be checked against AgroCloud's own fields. Because the guide predates the July 2026 redesign, a short logged-in walkthrough of a free EOS account is the only way to confirm layout details such as the rail, chips and notification panel. That is optional: AgroCloud needs EOS's functions, not its exact layout.
